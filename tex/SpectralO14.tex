  \begin{abstract}
    Papers O12--O13 established that the exact Weil-block capacity
    exponent $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}(q)$ decreases monotonically from $4.80$ to $3.59$ as $q$
    ranges over $\{29,61,101,151,211\}$, refuting the finite-size hypothesis S1 and ruling out
    convergence toward the phenomenological target $\delta \in [7.4,10.6]$.
    The tension between $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ and the window
    $\beta^{*} \in (0.09,0.13)$ is therefore not a numerical artefact.
    Source~S2 in O13 identified an observable-class mismatch: the proxy-level
    $\delta \mapsto \beta^{*}$ prescription does
    not account for the block-normalisation by $|S_n|$, the internal block
    dimension $q$, or the central-phase contribution $\psi_c(\gamma + bx)$ present in every exact Weil fingerprint.
    We identify that mismatch precisely: the O7 argument operates
    on a scalar global capacity, while $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ is extracted from a
    block-by-block mean over representations of internal dimension $q$.
    We decompose the exact observable as
    $\Sigma_n^{(c)} = \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{geom}} + \Sigma_n^{\gamma}$,
    and isolate the central-phase contribution.
    A factor $q^{-\eta}$ is constant in an intra-$q$ regression and therefore cannot change
    its fitted slope; the earlier subtraction $\eta\log q/\log n^{*}$ belongs only to an
    endpoint or inter-$q$ estimator.
    Conditional on importing the LPS capacity-to-rate prescription, that estimator defines the
    phenomenological diagnostic
    \[
      \beta_{\mathrm{diag}} = \frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{end}} + \tfrac{1}{2}} + \epsilon(q),
    \]
    where $\epsilon(q)$ remains a modelled variance term.
    This is not a native Heisenberg derivation of $\beta^{*}$.
    The numerical diagnostic at $q \in \{29,61,101,151,211\}$ selects the paper's S2-B branch;
    the native transfer obstruction is independent of that numerical outcome.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  The Cosmochrony framework~\cite{Cosmochrony,Beau2026c} derives particle-physics observables from
  spectral properties of relational graphs associated with binary polyhedral subgroups of
  $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.
  Within the admissibility sub-programme, a central chain of papers---O3
  through O7~\cite{Beau2026a7,Beau2026a8,Beau2026a9,Beau2026a10,Beau2026a11}---proposes, on a
  changing-degree LPS family, the relation
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:beta_delta}
    \beta^{*} = \frac{1}{\delta + \tfrac{1}{2}},
  \end{equation}
  linking the cascade exponent $\beta^{*}$ to the capacity decay exponent $\delta$.
  Charged-lepton mass ratios constrain $\beta^{*} \in (0.09, 0.13)$~\cite{Beau2026a7}; importing
  \eqref{eq:beta_delta} then induces the target $\delta \in [7.4, 10.6]$.
  The equation is not a native consequence of the fixed-degree Heisenberg Cayley cascade:
  the corpus supplies neither its changing valence nor a growth process carrying the pair
  observable~\cite{Beau2026sgn}.

  Papers O8--O13~\cite{Beau2026a12,Beau2026a13,Beau2026a14,Beau2026a15,Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17}
  develop the computational machinery to measure $\delta$ on Heisenberg graphs
  $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ via exact Weil-block projective capacity.
  O13~\cite{Beau2026a17} establishes that $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}(q)$ decreases
  monotonically with $q$, with no extrapolation of the five-prime dataset producing
  $\delta_{\infty} > 5.0$.
  The finite-size hypothesis S1 (``larger primes will push $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ into
  $[7.4,10.6]$'') is ruled out.

  O13 identifies Source~S2 as an explanation of the tension: the proposed relation
  \eqref{eq:beta_delta} was formulated at the proxy level and does not apply natively to
  the exact Weil-block observable.
  Two specific gaps are named in O13, Section~8.3:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Normalisation gap.}
    $\Sigma_n^{(c)}$ is normalised by the BFS shell size $|S_n|$, not by the block
    dimension $q$.
    The O7 derivation absorbs this normalisation at the proxy level; its effect in the
    exact-block setting is not derived.
    \item \textbf{Central-phase contribution.}
    The fingerprint phase $\psi_c(\gamma + bx)$ mixes the central coordinate $\gamma$ and the
    lateral coordinate $b$ in a block-dependent way.
    The average over generic blocks $c$ that defines $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ carries a
    contribution from this phase that is absent from the proxy-level derivation of
    \eqref{eq:beta_delta}.
  \end{enumerate}

  The present paper resolves the intra-$q$ observable bookkeeping in S2 and records the remaining
  transfer boundary.
  \textbf{S1 is not revisited here}: it was closed definitively by O13, and all data and arguments
  pertaining to S1 are taken as established.
  O14 has a single logical target: explaining why the proxy-level prescription
  \eqref{eq:beta_delta} changes class when $\delta$ is replaced by
  $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$, and separating exact-block identities from conditional
  endpoint and cross-substrate diagnostics.

  \subsection{Logical status of the three central objects}
    \label{sec:logical_status}

    Three objects play distinct logical roles throughout this paper, and their hierarchy must
    be kept clear.

    \begin{description}
      \item[$\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$]
      The \emph{measured observable}: the power-law exponent extracted by log-log regression
      from the mean block capacity $\bar{\Sigma}_n$ on $G_q$, as defined and measured in
      O12--O13~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17}.
      It is an empirical quantity associated with a specific computational procedure (exact Weil
      projection, BFS on $G_q$, fitting window satisfying~(E1$'$)--(E3)).
      Its values $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}(q) \in [3.59, 4.80]$ for
      $q \in \{29,61,101,151,211\}$ are inputs to O14, not outputs.

      \item[$\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$]
      A legacy \emph{endpoint-normalised diagnostic}: $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ after
      subtraction of a modelled central-phase bias and
      $\eta \cdot \log q / \log n^{*}$.
      It is not the intra-$q$ OLS exponent: multiplication by $q^{-\eta}$ changes the intercept,
      not the slope in $\log n$.
      The normalisation exponent $\eta$ is a \emph{benchmark parameter}: its central value
      $\eta = 1/2$ is used in all numerical tables, but its analytical derivation from the
      metaplectic phase structure of $\rho_c$ is open direction O14-O1
      (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

      \item[$\beta^{*}$]
      The phenomenological cascade parameter entering the mass-ratio
      formula of O3~\cite{Beau2026a7}, constrained phenomenologically to
      $\beta^{*} \in (0.09, 0.13)$.
      It is not directly measured by O14.
      Applying \eqref{eq:beta_corrected} produces only a conditional diagnostic because the
      reciprocal law is imported from the LPS model.
    \end{description}

    The distinction between $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ (measured, procedure-dependent) and
    $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (an endpoint/inter-$q$ diagnostic) is central to the corrected
    interpretation of O14.

  \subsection{Main results}
    \label{sec:results_summary}

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item We exhibit the precise class of observable implicit in the O7 derivation and show it
      differs from the exact-block observable by two explicit corrections

      \item We derive the decomposition
      $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}} = \delta_{\mathrm{geom}} + \delta_{\gamma}$,

      \item We separate the exact intra-$q$ slope from the endpoint/inter-$q$ diagnostic and

      \item We evaluate the historical $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ diagnostic numerically and show that
      it selects S2-B
      (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}).
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Background and Notation}
  \label{sec:background}

  \subsection{Heisenberg graph and Weil blocks}
    \label{sec:heis}

    Fix an odd prime $q$.
    The Heisenberg group $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ has order $q^3$ and centre
    $Z(G_q) \cong \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$.
    An element is written $(a,b,\gamma)$ with $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ and
    $\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$.
    The $q-1$ faithful irreducible representations $\rho_c$
    ($c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$) each have dimension $q$.

    A \emph{Weil block} associated with $c$ is the set of Cayley graph generators
    $\{g : \rho_c(g) \neq 0\}$ acting on the representation space $V_c \cong \mathbb{C}^q$.
    The \emph{block-level projective capacity} at BFS depth $n$ is
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:sigma_def}
      \Sigma_n^{(c)} = \frac{\Delta r_n^{(c)}}{|S_n|},
    \end{equation}
    where $\Delta r_n^{(c)}$ is the incremental projective span in the $\rho_c$-representation and
    $|S_n|$ is the BFS shell size at depth $n$.
    The mean over blocks is $\bar{\Sigma}_n = \mathbb{E}_c[\Sigma_n^{(c)}]$.

  \subsection{Proxy-level capacity and the O7 derivation}
    \label{sec:o7}

    The O7 derivation~\cite{Beau2026a11} operates on a \emph{scalar global capacity}
    $C_n^{\mathrm{proxy}}$, defined via a Fourier-character proxy that abelianises the representation
    structure.
    The derivation yields \eqref{eq:beta_delta} under three implicit assumptions:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(A1)] The observable is a \emph{scalar} function of BFS depth $n$, independent of the
      representation label $c$.
      \item[(A2)] The normalisation is \emph{homogeneous}: the only length scale entering the
      normalisation is $|S_n|$.
      \item[(A3)] There is no \emph{internal structure} within the observable: no block dimension $q$,
      no phase mixing between $\gamma$ and $b$.
    \end{enumerate}

    All three assumptions hold for the Fourier-character proxy of O11~\cite{Beau2026a15}.
    None of them holds for the exact Weil observable $\Sigma_n^{(c)}$ measured in
    O12--O13~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17}.
    The failure of (A1)--(A3) in the exact setting constitutes the observable-class mismatch whose
    consequences we now make precise.

  \section{Observable-Class Mismatch}
  \label{sec:mismatch}

  \subsection{Failure of assumption (A1): block heterogeneity}
    \label{sec:a1}

    For the exact Weil observable, $\Sigma_n^{(c)}$ varies across blocks $c$ at fixed $n$.
    O12 Table~2 and O13 Table~3~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17} show that condition (E2), which
    requires block-by-block universality, is violated at $q \in \{29,53,61\}$ and satisfied only at
    $q \in \{101,151,211\}$ after the representation variety has been sufficiently sampled.

    Consequently, $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ is a property of the \emph{mean}
    $\bar{\Sigma}_n$, not of an individual scalar observable.
    The O7 argument operates on the latter; its extension to the former supplies only the scalar
    power-law premise and leaves the growth-model premises separate.

    \begin{proposition}[Scope of mean universality]
      \label{prop:mean_universality}
      Suppose $\bar{\Sigma}_n \sim C_0 \, n^{-\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ holds with $R^2 \geq 0.97$ over
      a fitting window satisfying condition~(E1$'$).
      Then the mean has a well-defined finite-window decay exponent.
      This discharges only the scalar power-law premise of the O7 argument.
      It does not supply the changing-degree growth equation, the multiplicative coupling of
      redundancy to its productive frontier, or the identification of the Heisenberg mean with
      the LPS capacity variable.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The assumed fit supplies the stated exponent for $\bar{\Sigma}_n$ by definition.
      The remaining premises concern a distinct growth model and do not follow from mean
      universality~\cite{Beau2026sgn}.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Failure of assumption (A2): normalisation by $q$}
    \label{sec:a2}

    The exact Weil representation $\rho_c$ has internal dimension $q$.
    The incremental span $\Delta r_n^{(c)}$ accumulates over the $q$-dimensional fibre of the
    representation.
    The normalisation in \eqref{eq:sigma_def} divides by $|S_n|$ (graph-level shell size) but not
    by $q$.

    Let $\tilde{\Sigma}_n^{(c)} = q^{-\alpha} \Sigma_n^{(c)}$ for some exponent $\alpha \geq 0$
    to be determined.
    The O7 derivation assumes $\alpha = 0$.
    In the exact-block setting, dimensional analysis of the Weil projection suggests $\alpha = 1$:
    each block spans a $q$-dimensional representation, so the effective capacity per representation
    degree of freedom is $\Sigma_n^{(c)}/q$.

    \begin{definition}[Block-normalised observable]
      \label{def:block_normalised}
      The \emph{block-normalised capacity} is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:sigma_norm}
        \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{norm}} = \frac{\Delta r_n^{(c)}}{|S_n|^{\alpha} \cdot q^{\eta}},
      \end{equation}
      where $\alpha \in [0,1]$ and $\eta \in [0,1]$ are structural exponents to be determined from
      the Weil projection geometry.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{proposition}[Fixed-$q$ slope invariance]
      \label{prop:fixed_q_invariance}
      At fixed $q$, multiplication of a positive observable by $q^{-\eta}$ leaves every OLS slope
      against $\log n$ unchanged.
      Hence a term $\eta\log q/\log n^{*}$ cannot be subtracted from the intra-$q$ fitted exponent
      $\hat\delta_{\mathrm{exact}}(q)$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      In logarithmic coordinates,
      $\log(q^{-\eta}\Sigma_n) = \log\Sigma_n-\eta\log q$.
      The second term is constant across the fitting window, so it changes only the regression
      intercept.
      A quotient by $\log n^{*}$ arises only in an endpoint estimator such as
      $-\log\Sigma_{n^{*}}/\log n^{*}$, or in a separately specified inter-$q$ estimator.
    \end{proof}

    The distinction is therefore estimator-dependent.
    The exact observable fixes the shell normalisation $\alpha=1$.
    A factor $q^{-\eta}$ may be useful for comparing amplitudes across primes, but it cannot
    correct an intra-$q$ slope.
    Defining an inter-$q$ estimator and determining $\eta$ for it remain open direction O14-O1
    (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

  \subsection{Failure of assumption (A3): central-phase contribution}
    \label{sec:a3}

    The three-step fingerprint of a block $c = (c_1, c_2, c_3)$ contains the phase factor
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:phase}
      \psi_c(\gamma_k + b_k x) = e^{2\pi i c \gamma_k / q} \cdot e^{2\pi i c b_k x / q},
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma_k \in Z(G_q)$ is the central coordinate of the $k$-th generator and $b_k$ is the
    lateral coordinate.
    This phase is absent from the Fourier-character proxy, which abelianises $G_q$ and discards
    the central extension.

    The phase \eqref{eq:phase} has two distinct effects on $\Sigma_n^{(c)}$:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Transverse decoherence.}
      For generic blocks $c$, the phases $\psi_c(\gamma_k)$ are uniformly distributed mod~$q$,
      producing destructive interference in the incremental span.
      This accelerates the saturation of $\Delta r_n^{(c)}$ relative to the proxy, increasing
      $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$ above $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{proxy}}$.
      \item \textbf{Block heterogeneity.}
      The phase contribution varies with $c$, producing the inter-block variance observed in
      \item O12--O13~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17}.
      As $q$ grows, the phase space of $\psi_c$ becomes more uniformly sampled, and the variance
      decreases---consistent with the O13 finding that condition~(E2) is satisfied at larger~$q$.
    \end{enumerate}

  \section{Decomposition of the Exact Observable}
  \label{sec:decomposition}

  \begin{definition}[Geometric and central-phase components]
    \label{def:decomp}
    Write
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:decomp}
      \Sigma_n^{(c)} = \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{geom}} + \Sigma_n^{\gamma},
    \end{equation}
    where $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{geom}}$ is the contribution from the lateral coordinates $(a,b)$ alone
    (as in the proxy), and $\Sigma_n^{\gamma}$ is the additional contribution from the central
    coordinate $\gamma$ via the phase \eqref{eq:phase}.
  \end{definition}

  The decomposition \eqref{eq:decomp} is well-defined because the Weil projection decomposes
  $V_c = V_c^{\mathrm{lat}} \oplus V_c^{\mathrm{cen}}$ into a lateral subspace (spanned by
  generators with $\gamma = 0$) and a central subspace (spanned by generators with
  $\gamma \neq 0$).

  \begin{proposition}[Decay exponent decomposition]
    \label{prop:exponent_decomp}
    Suppose $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{geom}} \sim A \, n^{-\delta_{\mathrm{geom}}}$ and
    $\Sigma_n^{\gamma} \sim B \, n^{-\delta_{\gamma}}$ with
    $\delta_{\gamma} > \delta_{\mathrm{geom}}$.
    Then the mean observable satisfies
    $\bar{\Sigma}_n \sim A \, n^{-\delta_{\mathrm{geom}}}$ asymptotically,
    with a correction of order $B/A \cdot n^{-(\delta_{\gamma} - \delta_{\mathrm{geom}})}$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{remark}
    Proposition~\ref{prop:exponent_decomp} implies that $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}$
    overestimates $\delta_{\mathrm{geom}}$ in finite fitting windows, since the faster-decaying
    central-phase component $\Sigma_n^{\gamma}$ steepens the observed slope.
    The effective exponent in the fitting window is
    $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}} = \delta_{\mathrm{geom}} + \delta_{\gamma,\mathrm{corr}}$,
    where $\delta_{\gamma,\mathrm{corr}} > 0$ is the finite-window bias from $\Sigma_n^{\gamma}$.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{proposition}[Central-phase bias]
    \label{prop:gamma_bias}
    For the Heisenberg group $G_q$ with $q$ prime, the central-phase component satisfies
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:gamma_bias}
      \delta_{\gamma,\mathrm{corr}} \approx \frac{\log(1 + q^{-1/2})}{\log n^{*}},
    \end{equation}
    where $n^{*}$ is the saturation depth.
    As $q \to \infty$, $\delta_{\gamma,\mathrm{corr}} \to 0$, and
    $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}} \to \delta_{\mathrm{geom}}$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The central-phase contribution to the incremental span at depth $n$ is bounded by
$|\Sigma_n^{\gamma}| \leq C \, q^{-1/2}$ by the Weil bound on character sums.
For a power-law fit over the window $[n_0, n^{*}]$, the contribution of a term of order
$q^{-1/2}$ to the slope in log-log space is of order $\log(1 + q^{-1/2})/\log n^{*}$.
  \end{proof}

  \section{Conditional Reciprocal Diagnostic}
  \label{sec:corrected_relation}

  \begin{proposition}[Conditional endpoint diagnostic]
    \label{thm:corrected}
    Assume all of the following:
    (i) the LPS capacity-to-rate prescription \eqref{eq:beta_delta} is imported as a
    cross-substrate hypothesis;
    (ii) $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is defined by an endpoint or specified inter-$q$ estimator rather
    than the intra-$q$ OLS slope; and
    (iii) the central-phase and block-variance models used below hold.
    Then the historical O14 bookkeeping defines the phenomenological diagnostic
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:beta_corrected}
      \beta_{\mathrm{diag}}(q)
      = \frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}(q) + \tfrac{1}{2}} + \epsilon(q),
    \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:delta_eff}
      \delta_{\mathrm{eff}}(q) = \hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}(q)
      - \delta_{\gamma,\mathrm{corr}}(q)
      - \eta \cdot \frac{\log q}{\log n^{*}(q)},
    \end{equation}
    and $\epsilon(q)=O(V_n^{\max}(q))$ is a modelled block-variance term.
    The formula does not identify $\beta_{\mathrm{diag}}$ with the native Heisenberg span-growth
    exponent or establish $\beta^{*}$.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    Under premise~(i), substitution of the endpoint definition \eqref{eq:delta_eff} into the
    imported reciprocal law gives \eqref{eq:beta_corrected}; premise~(iii) appends the stated
    variance term.
    Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed_q_invariance} shows why this is not a correction of the intra-$q$
    regression slope.
    None of these algebraic substitutions supplies a native Heisenberg growth equation.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}
    In the endpoint diagnostic, \eqref{eq:delta_eff} lowers
    $\hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}}(q)$ by both modelled terms.
    At fixed $q$, only the central-phase component can affect the fitted slope; the factor
    $q^{-\eta}$ changes its intercept.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Pipeline-Based Evaluation of the Historical Diagnostic}
  \label{sec:numerical}

  \subsection{Status of this section}
    \label{sec:numerical_status}

    The results reported here are obtained from the real BFS pipeline of O12/O13,
    using the companion script \texttt{SpectralO14\_simulation.py} in MODE~B.
    For each prime $q$, the script loads the file \texttt{q$\langle q\rangle$\_o12.npz}
    produced by \texttt{generate\_O12\_npz.py} and extracts:
    $\hat\delta_{\mathrm{exact}}$ (OLS fit from O12),
    $C(q) = \bar\ell_\gamma$ (mean central-coordinate coherence over the fitting window
    $[n_0,n_1]$ from O13~\cite{Beau2026a17}),
    and the actual fitting-window bounds $n_0$, $n_1$.

    The pipeline provides a direct measurement of the shell-level central-coordinate
    coherence but does not expose block-level phase vectors, which are consumed
    internally by Gram--Schmidt in O12.
    The distinction between shell-level and block-level phase observables, and its
    consequence for ansatz~[H1], is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_results}.

  \subsection{Computation of corrections}
    \label{sec:corrections}

    For each prime $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$ we compute the following quantities
    from the pipeline data.

    \paragraph{Phase coherence and circular variance.}
      The phase coherence factor is the mean central-coordinate coherence over the
      fitting window:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:Cq_def}
        C(q) = \bar\ell_\gamma = \frac{1}{n_1 - n_0 + 1}\sum_{n=n_0}^{n_1} \ell_\gamma(n),
      \end{equation}
      where $\ell_\gamma(n) = |\langle e^{2\pi i\gamma/q}\rangle_{S_n}|$ is the
      shell-level central-coordinate coherence measured by O12~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
      The circular variance is $\mathrm{Var}(\theta)(q) = -2\log C(q)$.
      No Gaussian approximation enters this formula.

    \paragraph{Central-phase bias.}
      The ansatz~[H1]
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:delta_gamma_ansatz}
        \delta_{\gamma}(q) \approx \frac{\mathrm{Var}(\theta)(q)}{2\log q}
      \end{equation}
      is applied with the circular variance computed from $\bar\ell_\gamma$ above.
      As shown in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_results}, this ansatz cannot be tested
      directly from the pipeline because $\bar\ell_\gamma$ is a shell-level quantity,
      not a block-level phase bias.

    \paragraph{Endpoint-normalisation diagnostic.}
      With benchmark $\eta = 1/2$~[H3] and the actual fitting-window depth $n_1$ from O13:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:norm_corr}
        \text{endpoint term} = \eta \cdot \frac{\log q}{\log n_1}.
      \end{equation}
      This uses the real $n_1 \in \{5, 7, 10, 12, 13\}$ for
      $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$ respectively, yielding endpoint terms in
      $[1.00, 1.06]$, roughly twice the analytical approximation of $0.5$.
      By Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed_q_invariance}, these numbers do not correct the
      intra-$q$ OLS slopes.

    \paragraph{Legacy endpoint exponent and reciprocal diagnostic.}
      $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}} = \hat{\delta}_{\mathrm{exact}} - \delta_{\gamma} -
      \text{endpoint term}$,
      and $\beta_{\mathrm{diag}} = 1/(\delta_{\mathrm{eff}} + 1/2)$.
      These columns reproduce the historical O14 diagnostic; they are not native fitted
      exponents or derived cascade parameters.

  \subsection{Benchmark scaling for the inter-$q$ exponent $\eta$}
  \label{subsec:eta-derivation}

  This subsection records the scaling motivation for the benchmark $\eta=1/2$.
  It does not derive an intra-$q$ slope correction or uniquely determine an inter-$q$ estimator.

  \paragraph{Block decomposition.}
    Write the exact observable as a sum over Weil blocks:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:O_sum}
      \mathcal{O}(q) = \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}(q)} A_b(q)\,e^{i\theta_b(q)},
      \qquad |\mathcal{B}(q)| \sim q.
    \end{equation}

  \paragraph{Weil amplitude constraint.}
    By the Weil bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma_bias}), each block amplitude satisfies
    $|A_b(q)| \lesssim q^{-1/2}$, with saturation for a dense subset of blocks.
    Write $A_b(q) = q^{-1/2}\tilde{A}_b(q)$ with $\tilde{A}_b = O(1)$.

  \paragraph{Central-phase decorrelation as a biased CLT.}
    The phases $\theta_b(q)$ are governed by the central coordinate $\gamma$ and exhibit
    bounded correlations controlled by the Weil bound.
    This places the sum in the regime of a central limit theorem with bounded dependence
    and a systematic bias $\delta_{\gamma} \geq 0$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:clt_biased}
      \left|\sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}(q)} \tilde{A}_b(q)\,e^{i\theta_b(q)}\right|
      \sim q^{1/2 - \delta_{\gamma}}.
    \end{equation}
    Combined with $A_b \sim q^{-1/2}$, the unnormalised observable scales as
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:O_scaling}
      \mathcal{O}(q) \sim q^{-1/2} \cdot q^{1/2-\delta_{\gamma}} = q^{-\delta_{\gamma}}.
    \end{equation}

  \paragraph{Benchmarking $\eta$.}
    The block-normalised observable is $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{norm}}(q) = q^{-\eta}\mathcal{O}(q)$,
    and the effective exponent definition requires
    $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{norm}}(q) \sim q^{-\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}}$.
    Choosing the square-root benchmark gives
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:eta_fixed}
      \eta = \tfrac{1}{2}.
    \end{equation}
    The value $\eta = 1/2$ reflects a square-root balance: Weil amplitudes
    scale as $q^{-1/2}$, block count grows as $q$, and central-phase decorrelation reduces
    coherent accumulation from $q^{1/2}$ to $q^{1/2-\delta_{\gamma}}$.

  \paragraph{Status.}
    This is a modelling benchmark, not a uniqueness result: equation~\eqref{eq:O_scaling} gives
    $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}=\delta_\gamma+\eta$ once an inter-$q$ normalisation is chosen, but does
    not select $\eta$ by itself.
    A specified inter-$q$ estimator and mixing estimates for the phases $\theta_b(q)$ are left as
    open direction O14-O1 (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

  \subsection{Connection between $\delta_{\gamma}$, $\epsilon(q)$, and phase variance}
  \label{subsec:eps_connection}

  \paragraph{$\epsilon(q)$ as the observable imprint of projection residuals.}
    In the Cosmochrony framework~\cite{Cosmochrony}, the non-injectivity of the projection
    $\Pi : \Omega \to O$ induces irreducible residual fluctuations at the observable level.
    In the spectral setting, these fluctuations manifest as phase decorrelation across
    Weil blocks, controlled by the central coordinate $\gamma$.

    Conditional on the reciprocal model, the residual term $\epsilon(q)$ in
    \eqref{eq:beta_corrected} is modelled as the propagation of projection residuals:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:eps_F}
      \epsilon(q) = \mathcal{F}\!\left(\delta_{\gamma}(q)\right) - \delta_{\gamma}(q),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{F}$ encodes the assumed nonlinear propagation of the phase bias through
    the reciprocal diagnostic.

  \paragraph{Phase-variance relation.}
    Define the phase coherence factor $C(q)$ as in \eqref{eq:Cq_def}.
    From the biased CLT, $\mathcal{O}(q) \sim C(q)$.
    Matching with a power-law $\mathcal{O}(q) \sim q^{-\delta_{\gamma}(q)}$ yields the
    ansatz~[H1]:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:delta_gamma_var}
      \delta_{\gamma}(q) \approx \frac{\mathrm{Var}(\theta)(q)}{2\log q}.
    \end{equation}
    Within this ansatz, bounded phase variance gives $\epsilon(q)\to0$;
    slowly growing variance gives decay; and anomalous variance growth gives a persistent term.

  \paragraph{Consequence.}
    Within the legacy diagnostic, the pair $(\delta_{\gamma}(q),\eta)$ determines the displayed
    endpoint displacement.
    It does not determine a native capacity-to-rate law.

  \subsection{Pipeline validation results and interpretation}
  \label{sec:pipeline_results}

  Table~\ref{tab:phase_variance} reports the statistics obtained from the real BFS
  pipeline of O12/O13 (MODE B).
  Three findings are immediate.

  \paragraph{Finding 1: high central-coordinate coherence in the fitting window.}
    The phase coherence factor $C(q) \in [0.92, 0.96]$ throughout
    (panel~(F) of Figure~\ref{fig:validation}).
    This means that the central coordinate $\gamma$ remains highly coherent within
    the BFS fitting window $[n_0, n_1]$: the shell-level average
    $\ell_\gamma(n) = |\langle e^{2\pi i \gamma/q}\rangle_{S_n}|$ is near unity,
    indicating that $\gamma$ has not yet diversified in the pre-saturation regime.
    This is consistent with the O12 finding (Section~5 of~\cite{Beau2026a16}) that
    the coherence length $\ell_\gamma(n)$ decays only slowly in the fitting window.

  \paragraph{Finding 2: negligible central-phase bias.}
    Since $C(q) \approx 0.93$, the circular variance satisfies
    $\mathrm{Var}(\theta)(q) \in [0.09, 0.16]$, which via the ansatz~[H1] gives
    $\delta_\gamma \in [0.008, 0.023]$.
    This is negligible relative to $\hat\delta_{\mathrm{exact}} \in [3.6, 4.8]$.
    \textbf{The modelled central-phase bias $\delta_\gamma$ is not the dominant displacement in
    the legacy pipeline diagnostic.}

  \paragraph{Finding 3: endpoint term dominates the legacy diagnostic.}
    The term $\eta \cdot \log q/\log n_1$ evaluates to $\approx 1.03$--$1.06$,
    using the actual fitting-window depths $n_1 \in [5, 13]$ extracted from O13.
    This is roughly twice the value $0.5$ produced by the analytical approximation
    $n^* \approx q$ in MODE~A.
    The endpoint term is therefore the dominant numerical displacement in the legacy
    diagnostic.
    It is computed from the fitting-window endpoint but is not a correction to the intra-$q$
    regression slope.

  \paragraph{Interpretation.}
    The pipeline results reveal a conceptual refinement of the O14 theory.
    The quantity $\ell_\gamma(n)$ measured by O12 is a \emph{shell-level} observable:
    it captures how uniformly $\gamma$ is distributed across the BFS shell $S_n$.
    The central-phase bias $\delta_\gamma$ predicted by the theory of
    Section~\ref{sec:decomposition} operates at the \emph{block level}: it enters
    through the phase $\psi_c(\gamma + bx)$ acting on the Weil representation
    $\rho_c$ for a specific block $c$.
    These two observables are distinct, and $\ell_\gamma(n)$ is not a direct
    proxy for the block-level phase bias.
    This distinction is identified as open direction O14-O4
    (Section~\ref{sec:open}): a block-level phase observable, not accessible
    from the current O12 pipeline outputs, is required to test ansatz~[H1] directly.

  \paragraph{What the pipeline validates.}
    Despite the above, the pipeline provides two concrete results.
    First, the legacy endpoint diagnostic gives $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}<5.0$ for all
    $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$.
    Second, the term $\eta\log q/\log n_1$ dominates that diagnostic, while the current pipeline
    cannot measure the block-level phase bias directly.
    The underlying intra-$q$ OLS exponents remain the reported
    $\hat\delta_{\mathrm{exact}}$ values.

  \subsection{Two scenarios}
  \label{sec:scenarios}

  \paragraph{Scenario S2-A (phenomenological alignment).}
    If a specified inter-$q$ estimator stabilised above $7.0$, the imported reciprocal
    prescription would place $\beta_{\mathrm{diag}}$ in $(0.09,0.13)$.
    Such alignment would be numerical only; it would not construct a native Heisenberg
    capacity-to-rate bridge.

  \paragraph{Scenario S2-B (Structural revision).}
    The legacy endpoint diagnostic remains below $5.0$ in the pipeline data, so the
    observable-class bookkeeping does not close the numerical gap.
    The amplification mechanism of O3~\cite{Beau2026a7} would then require modification
    in the exact-block setting, or the link between mass ratios and spectral capacity would
    require a different structural interpretation.

  \section{Interpretive Consequences}
  \label{sec:physical}

  \subsection{Scenario S2-A: conditional alignment only}
    \label{sec:s2a}

    Even under S2-A, the observable-class mismatch would explain only the numerical tension inside
    the imported reciprocal prescription.
    It would not derive the cascade exponent from first principles.
    The generation structure established in SpectralStratigraphy~\cite{Beau2026a4} is unaffected
    by this distinction.

  \subsection{Scenario S2-B: required revisions}
    \label{sec:s2b}

    Under S2-B, at least one of the following modifications is required:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item The amplification mechanism of O3~\cite{Beau2026a7} must be rederived in the exact-block
      setting, potentially yielding a different functional form for the
      $\delta \mapsto \beta^{*}$ map.
      \item The identification of $\beta^{*}$ with the charged-lepton cascade exponent requires
      reexamination, possibly via a sector-dependent exponent.
      \item The link between the Heisenberg graph capacity and the LPS-graph projective resolution
      (established in O1--O2~\cite{Beau2026a5,Beau2026a6}) must be revised to account for the
      central-phase degree of freedom.
    \end{enumerate}

  \subsection{Quark and neutrino sectors}
    \label{sec:quarks}

    Conditional use of \eqref{eq:beta_corrected} assigns a modelled term $\epsilon(q)$ depending

    Extension to the quark and neutrino sectors requires computing the block variance at the
    appropriate $q$ values for those sectors, which is left as open direction O14-O2
    (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

  \section{Open Directions}
  \label{sec:open}

  \paragraph{O14-O1 (Analytical derivation of $\eta$).}
    Define an inter-$q$ estimator before assigning a normalisation exponent.
    The value $\eta=1/2$ is a square-root benchmark motivated by Weil amplitudes and block count,
    not an intra-$q$ slope correction or a uniqueness theorem.
    A derivation requires both the estimator definition and mixing estimates for the phases
    $\theta_b(q)$ across Weil blocks.

  \paragraph{O14-O2 (Quark and neutrino exponents).}
    The conditional diagnostic \eqref{eq:beta_corrected} may be evaluated for the block
    structures associated with the quark and neutrino representations.
    This requires identifying the appropriate Weil-block decomposition for those sectors and
    computing $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at the corresponding primes.

  \paragraph{O14-O3 (Analytical derivation of $\delta_{\gamma,\mathrm{corr}}$).}
    Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma_bias} provides an estimate of the central-phase bias via the
    Weil bound.
    A tighter derivation tracking the exact phase-space average of
    $\psi_c(\gamma + bx)$ over generic blocks would sharpen the correction and remove the
    dependence on the phenomenological ansatz~[H1].

  \paragraph{O14-O4 (Block-level phase observable).}
    The BFS pipeline validation reveals that $\ell_\gamma(n)$, the shell-level
    central-coordinate coherence measured by O12, is not a direct proxy for the
    block-level phase bias $\delta_\gamma$ of ansatz~[H1].
    The relevant phase decorrelation operates at the level of individual Weil blocks
    $\rho_c$, through the phase $\psi_c(\gamma + bx)$ acting on the $q$-dimensional
    representation.
    Extracting this block-level phase observable requires storing the complex
    fingerprint vectors $z_b^{(c)}(n)$ per block before Gram-Schmidt orthogonalisation,
    which the current O12 pipeline discards.
    Modifying O12 to save these intermediate complex values would provide the direct
    test of ansatz~[H1] and definitively discriminate the block-level $\delta_\gamma$
    from the shell-level $\ell_\gamma(n)$.

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  We have partially resolved Source~S2 of O13~\cite{Beau2026a17} by establishing the precise
  observable-class mismatch between the proxy-level O7 derivation~\cite{Beau2026a11} and
  the exact Weil-block observable measured in O12--O13~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17}.
  The mismatch has three components:
  block heterogeneity (failure of~A1), normalisation by the block dimension $q$
  (failure of~A2), and the central-phase contribution $\psi_c(\gamma + bx)$
  (failure of~A3).

  The exact intra-$q$ result is Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed_q_invariance}: a factor
  $q^{-\eta}$ changes the intercept and cannot change the fitted decay exponent.
  The term $\eta\log q/\log n_1$ therefore belongs to an endpoint or inter-$q$ estimator whose
  full definition remains open.
  Equation~\eqref{eq:beta_corrected} is retained only as a phenomenological diagnostic under the
  imported LPS reciprocal prescription; it is not a native Heisenberg relation.

  The BFS pipeline of O12/O13 was run for all five primes and provides three
  concrete results (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_results}).
  The shell-level central-coordinate coherence $\bar\ell_\gamma \approx 0.93$
  is near unity within the fitting window, confirming that $\gamma$ does not yet
  diversify in the pre-saturation regime; the central-phase bias $\delta_\gamma$ is
  therefore negligible.
  The dominant displacement in the legacy endpoint diagnostic is
  $\eta\log q/\log n_1 \approx 1.03$--$1.06$.
  It does not alter the O12/O13 intra-$q$ slopes.
  The diagnostic remains below $5.0$ for all tested primes, selecting the historical S2-B branch.

  A conceptual distinction revealed by the pipeline is that $\ell_\gamma(n)$ is a
  shell-level observable, whereas the block-level phase bias $\delta_\gamma$ of
  ansatz~[H1] operates at the level of individual Weil representations $\rho_c$.
  Testing [H1] directly requires storing the intermediate complex fingerprint vectors
  before Gram--Schmidt orthogonalisation (open direction O14-O4).

  O14 therefore supplies a partial intra-$q$ resolution of the observable-class mismatch and
  isolates the remaining estimator layer.
  It does not close the cross-substrate capacity-to-rate transfer, which is refuted as a native
  composite for the corpus-defined Heisenberg objects~\cite{Beau2026sgn}.
